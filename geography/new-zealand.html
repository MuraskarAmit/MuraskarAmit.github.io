New Zealand: map and facts

New Zealand is in Oceania. It has about 5.32 million people (2025), the 122nd largest population. It covers 267,710 km², the 74th largest by area. That is about 20 people per km². Its highest point is Aoraki (Mount Cook), at 3,724 m. The longest river on its map is the Waikato, 425 km from source to mouth.

Facts

  • Population: 5,324,700 (2025, World Bank)
  • Area: 267,710 km² (2024, World Bank)
  • Capital: Wellington
  • Highest point: Aoraki (Mount Cook), 3,724 m (Wikidata)
  • Forest: 38.9% of the land (2024, World Bank)

Rivers

The longest rivers on the map of New Zealand, with the length of each whole river (Wikidata):

  • Waikato: 425 km
  • Whanganui: 290 km
  • Taieri: 288 km
  • Mataura: 240 km
  • Rangitikei: 240 km
  • Waiau: 217 km
  • Clarence: 209 km
  • Manawatu: 180 km
  • Ngaruroro: 174 km
  • Mohaka: 172 km

Also on the map: Buller, Wairau, Waiau, Whangaehu, Waimakariri, Kawarau, Tongariro, Clutha, Oreti and Hawea.

Mountains and landscapes

Its highest peaks:

  • Aoraki (Mount Cook): 3,724 m (its highest point)
  • Tapuae-o-Uenuku: 2,885 m
  • Mount Ruapehu: 2,797 m
  • Mount Taranaki: 2,518 m
  • Mount Anglem: 980 m

Mountain ranges: Southern Alps.

Plateaus, plains, deserts and other regions: Polynesia, Auckland Islands, Banks Peninsula, Coromandel Peninsula, Fiordland, Marlborough Sounds, North Island Volcanic Plateau, Otago Peninsula, Chatham Islands and Kermadec Islands.

Land

Land cover seen from satellites (NASA MODIS, 2024): grassland 33%, evergreen broadleaf forest 28.6%, savanna 15.9%, evergreen conifer forest 12.5%, water 2.9%, wooded savanna 2.3%.

Landforms, worked out from the height of the land (USGS GMTED2010): mountains 52.3%, hills 38%, plains 9.3%.

Forests covered 38.9% of its land in 2024, up from 35.6% in 1990 (World Bank).
