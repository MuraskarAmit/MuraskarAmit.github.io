Antarctica: map and facts

Antarctica covers 14,200,000 km². Its highest point is Vinson Massif, at 4,892 m.

Facts

  • Area: 14,200,000 km² (CIA World Factbook (archived)). This figure covers a different area from the one this map shows: as drawn, about 12,300,000 km².
  • Highest point: Vinson Massif, 4,892 m (Wikidata)

Mountains and landscapes

Its highest peaks:

  • Vinson Massif: 4,892 m (its highest point)
  • Mount Kirkpatrick: 4,528 m
  • Mount Sidley: 4,285 m
  • Mount Kaplan: 4,230 m
  • Mount Minto: 4,165 m
  • Mount Fridtjof Nansen: 4,070 m
  • Mount Lister: 4,025 m
  • Faure Peak: 3,940 m

Mountain ranges: Transantarctic Mountains, Ellsworth Mountains, Pensacola Mountains, Queen Maud Mountains, Admiralty Mountains, Flood Range, Ford Ranges, Napier Mountains, Prince Albert Mountains, Prince Charles Mountains, Queen Alexandra Range and Scott Mountains.

Plateaus, plains, deserts and other regions: Antarctic Peninsula, East Antarctica, Polar Plateau, West Antarctica, American Highland, Dome A (Argus), Dome C (Charlie), Dome F (Fuji), Hollick-Kenyon Plateau, Marie Byrd Land, Queen Maud Land and Rockefeller Plateau.

Land

Land cover seen from satellites (NASA MODIS, 2024): snow and ice 99.9%.

Landforms, worked out from the height of the land (USGS GMTED2010): plateaus 84.4%, hills 10.8%, mountains 2.7%, plains 1.9%.

Neighbours

Antarctica has no land borders on this map.
