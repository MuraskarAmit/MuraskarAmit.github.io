Australia: map and facts

Australia is in Oceania. It has about 27.6 million people (2025), the 55th largest population. It covers 7,741,220 km², the 6th largest by area. That is about 3.6 people per km². Its highest point is Mount Kosciuszko, at 2,228 m. The longest river on its map is the Murray, 2,508 km from source to mouth.

Facts

  • Population: 27,614,411 (2025, World Bank)
  • Area: 7,741,220 km² (2024, World Bank)
  • Capital: Canberra
  • Highest point: Mount Kosciuszko, 2,228 m (Wikidata)
  • Forest: 17.4% of the land (2024, World Bank)

Mountains and landscapes

Its highest peaks:

  • Mount Kosciuszko: 2,228 m (its highest point)
  • Mount Bogong: 1,986 m
  • Mount Buller: 1,805 m
  • Mount Ossa: 1,617 m
  • Mount Bartle Frere: 1,611 m
  • Round Mountain: 1,586 m
  • Barrington Tops: 1,555 m
  • Mount Zeil: 1,531 m

Mountain ranges: Great Dividing Range, Australian Alps, Blue Mountains, Darling Range, Flinders Ranges, Gregory Range, Hamersley Range, MacDonnell Ranges and Selwyn Range.

Plateaus, plains, deserts and other regions: Western Plateau, Great Artesian Basin, Arnhem Land, Cape York Peninsula, Great Sandy Desert, Great Victoria Desert, Murray–Darling Basin, Nullarbor Plain, Gibson Desert, Kimberley Plateau, Riverina and Atherton Tableland.

Land

Land cover seen from satellites (NASA MODIS, 2024): open shrubland 53.6%, grassland 26.1%, savanna 5.7%, farmland 3.9%, dense shrubland 3.6%, evergreen broadleaf forest 2.9%.

Landforms, worked out from the height of the land (USGS GMTED2010): plains 54.7%, plateaus 31.6%, hills 11.6%, mountains 2.1%.

Forests covered 17.4% of its land in 2024, down from 17.5% in 1990 (World Bank).
