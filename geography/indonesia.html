Indonesia: map and facts

Indonesia is in South-Eastern Asia. It has about 286 million people (2025), the 4th largest population. It covers 1,916,907 km², the 14th largest by area. That is about 149 people per km². Its highest point is Puncak Jaya, at 4,884 m. The longest river on its map is the Kapuas, 1,143 km from source to mouth.

Facts

  • Population: 285,721,236 (2025, World Bank)
  • Area: 1,916,907 km² (2024, World Bank)
  • Capital: Jakarta
  • Highest point: Puncak Jaya, 4,884 m (Wikidata)
  • Forest: 50.7% of the land (2024, World Bank)

Rivers

The longest rivers on the map of Indonesia, with the length of each whole river (Wikidata):

  • Kapuas: 1,143 km
  • Sepik: 1,126 km
  • Fly: 1,050 km
  • Mahakam: 940 km
  • Barito: 900 km
  • Mamberamo: 700 km
  • Kayan: 576 km
  • Asahan: 150 km

Also on the map: Digul, Indragi, Taritatu, Baliem, Masi and Pembuang.

Mountains and landscapes

Its highest peaks:

  • Puncak Jaya: 4,884 m (its highest point)
  • Gunung Kerinci: 3,805 m
  • Gunung Rinjani: 3,726 m
  • Gunung Semeru: 3,676 m
  • Bulu Rantekombola: 3,455 m
  • Gunung Ciremay: 3,078 m
  • Fuyul Sojol: 3,030 m
  • Gunung Binaiya: 3,027 m

Mountain ranges: New Guinea Highlands, Barisan Mountains and Maoke Mountains.

Plateaus, plains, deserts and other regions: Malay Archipelago, Melanesia, Greater Sunda Islands, Moluccas, Lesser Sunda Islands, Aru Islands, Batu Islands, Batu Islands, Datu Peninsula, Riau Archipelago, Sangihe Islands and Talaud Islands.

Land

Land cover seen from satellites (NASA MODIS, 2024): evergreen broadleaf forest 65.2%, wooded savanna 17.9%, farmland with natural plants 4%, savanna 3.9%, farmland 2.7%, wetland 2.3%.

Landforms, worked out from the height of the land (USGS GMTED2010): plains 43%, mountains 33.6%, hills 22.3%, high mountains 1%.

Forests covered 50.7% of its land in 2024, down from 64.2% in 1990 (World Bank).
