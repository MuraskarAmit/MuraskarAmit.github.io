Canada: map and facts

Canada is in Northern America. It has about 41.7 million people (2025), the 38th largest population. It covers 9,894,508 km², the 2nd largest by area. That is about 4.2 people per km². Its highest point is Mount Logan, at 5,959 m. The longest river on its map is the Yukon, 3,190 km from source to mouth.

Facts

  • Population: 41,651,653 (2025, World Bank)
  • Area: 9,894,508 km² (area as drawn on this map)
  • Capital: Ottawa
  • Highest point: Mount Logan, 5,959 m (Wikidata)
  • Forest: 42% of the land (2024, World Bank)

Mountains and landscapes

Its highest peaks:

  • Mount Logan: 5,959 m (its highest point)
  • Mount Fairweather: 4,671 m
  • Mount Waddington: 4,019 m
  • Mount Robson: 3,954 m
  • Mount Sir Sandford: 3,519 m
  • Ulysses Mountain: 3,024 m
  • Keele Peak: 2,972 m

Mountain ranges: Rocky Mountains, Appalachian Mountains, Cascade Range, Coast Mountains, Mackenzie Mountains, Columbia Mountains, Selwyn Mountains, Torngat Mountains, Birch Mountains, Caribou Mountains, Cassiar Mountains and Ogilvie Mountains.

Plateaus, plains, deserts and other regions: Arctic Archipelago, Canadian Shield, Great Plains, Queen Elizabeth Islands, Barren Grounds, Parry Islands, Yukon Plateau, Cumberland Peninsula, Fraser Plateau, Gaspé Peninsula, Haida Gwaii and Laurentian Mountains.

Land

Land cover seen from satellites (NASA MODIS, 2024): grassland 20%, wooded savanna 18.4%, savanna 14.4%, evergreen conifer forest 10.4%, open shrubland 9.4%, mixed forest 6.7%.

Landforms, worked out from the height of the land (USGS GMTED2010): plains 33.4%, plateaus 28.6%, hills 21.7%, mountains 16.2%.

Forests covered 42% of its land in 2024, up from 40.2% in 1990 (World Bank).
