Peru: map and facts

Peru is in South America. It has about 34.6 million people (2025), the 48th largest population. It covers 1,285,216 km², the 19th largest by area. That is about 27 people per km². Its highest point is Nevado Huascaran, at 6,768 m. The longest river on its map is the Jurua, 3,100 km from source to mouth.

Facts

  • Population: 34,576,665 (2025, World Bank)
  • Area: 1,285,216 km² (2024, World Bank)
  • Capital: Lima
  • Highest point: Nevado Huascaran, 6,768 m (Wikidata)
  • Forest: 52.7% of the land (2024, World Bank)

Rivers

The longest rivers on the map of Peru, with the length of each whole river (Wikidata):

  • Jurua: 3,100 km
  • Maranón: 1,737 km
  • Putumayo: 1,610 km
  • Ucayali: 1,460 km
  • Napo: 1,130 km
  • Huallaga: 1,080 km
  • Madre de Dios: 1,060 km
  • Javari: 1,056 km
  • Santa: 347 km
  • Tumbes: 200 km

Also on the map: Pisco, Amazonas, Purus, Tambo, Ocona and Santiago.

Mountains and landscapes

Its highest peaks:

  • Nevado Huascaran: 6,768 m (its highest point)
  • Nevado Coropuna: 6,425 m
  • Nevado Auzangate: 6,384 m

Mountain ranges: Andes, Selvas, Cordillera Central, Cordillera Occidental and Cordillera Oriental.

Plateaus, plains, deserts and other regions: Amazon Basin and Altiplano.

Land

Land cover seen from satellites (NASA MODIS, 2024): evergreen broadleaf forest 56.1%, grassland 25.5%, bare ground 7.4%, open shrubland 3.1%, savanna 2.7%, wooded savanna 2.7%.

Landforms, worked out from the height of the land (USGS GMTED2010): plains 35.9%, high mountains 24.2%, mountains 22.3%, plateaus 11.1%, hills 6.5%.

Forests covered 52.7% of its land in 2024, down from 60.2% in 1990 (World Bank).
