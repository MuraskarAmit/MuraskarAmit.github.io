The lessons

Before its first questions, each of these levels shows a short lesson: an example to try, the method, and why it works.

1.1 Percent of a number

10% of a number is the number ÷ 10; 1% is ÷ 100. Build other percents from those: 25% of 640 is a quarter, 160; 15% is 10% + 5% (64 + 32 = 96).

Why it works: Percent means "out of 100", so 25% of 640 is 640 × 25 ÷ 100. Splitting into easy parts is quicker in your head.

1.2 Discounts

Work out the discount, then take it away: 25% off ₹480 is 480 − 120 = ₹360. Or pay the rest straight away: 25% off means you pay 75%.

Why it works: Both ways give the same price; use whichever percent is easier.

1.3 GST and tips

Add the tax to the price: ₹600 with 18% GST is 600 + 108 = ₹708. For 18%, take 10% (60) + 8% (48).

Why it works: GST rates in India are 5%, 12%, 18% and 28%; tips are often 10%, 15% or 20%.

1.4 Percentage change

Percentage change = change ÷ the old price × 100. From ₹80 to ₹100 is a change of 20, and 20 ÷ 80 = 25%.

Why it works: Always divide by where you started: from ₹100 down to ₹80 is a 20% drop, not 25%.

2.1 Squares to 30

For numbers near a round one, go up and down: 23² = 20 × 26 + 3² = 520 + 9 = 529. Squares ending in 5: 25² is 2 × 3 = 6, then 25: 625.

Why it works: (a − b)(a + b) = a² − b², so a² = (a − b)(a + b) + b². Knowing squares to 30 speeds up exam sums a lot.

2.2 Cubes to 15

A cube is the number times itself, three times: 12³ = 12 × 12 × 12 = 144 × 12 = 1,728. Learn 1³ to 10³ by heart (1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000).

Why it works: Cubes come up in volumes and in exam simplification questions.

2.3 Estimate

Round each number to an easy one, then multiply: 49.8 × 20.1 is about 50 × 20 = 1,000. Answers within 5% count.

Why it works: Estimating first catches big mistakes, and it's often all you need (bills, shopping).
