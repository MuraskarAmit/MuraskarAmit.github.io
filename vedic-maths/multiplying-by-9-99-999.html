Multiplying by 9, 99 or 999

एकन्यूनेन पूर्वेण Ekanyūnena Pūrveṇa “by one less than the one before”, a sutra of Vedic maths.

Ekanyūnena Pūrveṇa, “by one less than the one before”, multiplies a number by a row of nines in two quick steps, as long as the number has no more digits than there are nines.

The method

  1. Left part: one less than the number: 437 − 1 = 436.
  2. Right part: take the left part from the nines: 999 − 436 = 563. (That is all from 9 and the last from 10 of 437.)
  3. Put them side by side: 436563.
  4. If the number has fewer digits than the nines, put zeros in front first: 45 × 999 is 045 × 999.

Worked examples

437 × 999

  1. Left part: one less than the number.437 − 1 = 436
  2. Right part: take that from 999, digit by digit.999 − 436 = 563

437 × 999 = 436 | 563 = 436563

72 × 99

  1. Left part: one less than the number.72 − 1 = 71
  2. Right part: take that from 99, digit by digit.99 − 71 = 28

72 × 99 = 71 | 28 = 7128

45 × 999

  1. 45 has fewer digits than 999, so think of it as 045.045
  2. Left part: one less than the number.45 − 1 = 44
  3. Right part: take that from 999, digit by digit.999 − 044 = 955

45 × 999 = 44 | 955 = 44955

Why it works

Multiplying by 999 is multiplying by 1000 and taking the number away once: 437 × 999 = 437,000 − 437 = 436,000 + (1000 − 437) = 436,000 + 563.

Watch out

  • When the number has more digits than the nines, this simple form doesn’t apply. The book has a longer version, but 1234 × 99 is easier as 1234 × 100 − 1234 = 123,400 − 1234 = 122,166.
  • The right part keeps as many digits as there are nines: 98 × 999 is 097 | 902.
