Squaring numbers that end in 5

एकाधिकेन पूर्वेण Ekādhikena Pūrveṇa “by one more than the one before”, a sutra of Vedic maths.

Squaring a number that ends in 5 takes one small multiplication. Cover the 5, multiply what is left by one more than itself, and write 25 after it. The sutra behind it, Ekādhikena Pūrveṇa, says just that: “by one more than the one before”.

The method

  1. Cover the 5 and look at the number in front of it: 7 for 75, 11 for 115.
  2. Multiply that number by the next one up: 7 × 8 = 56.
  3. Write 25 after the result: 5625.

Worked examples

65²

  1. Cover the 5: 6 is left. One more than 6 is 7.6 × 7 = 42
  2. Write 25 after it.42 | 25

65² = 4225

95²

  1. Cover the 5: 9 is left. One more than 9 is 10.9 × 10 = 90
  2. Write 25 after it.90 | 25

95² = 9025

125²

  1. Cover the 5: 12 is left. One more than 12 is 13.12 × 13 = 156
  2. Write 25 after it.156 | 25

125² = 15625

Why it works

A number that ends in 5 is 10a + 5, where a is the part in front of the 5. Its square is (10a + 5)² = 100a² + 100a + 25 = 100 × a(a + 1) + 25. So the hundreds are a × (a + 1), and the last two digits are always 25.

Watch out

  • It only works for numbers that end in 5. For others, try squaring near a base or the duplex.
  • Write the 25 even when the product ends in 0: 105² is 10 × 11 = 110, then 25: 11025.
  • Bigger numbers just need a bigger multiplication: 245² is 24 × 25 = 600, then 25: 60025.
  • It works with a decimal point too: 7.5² = 56.25, because 7 × 8 = 56 and 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25.
