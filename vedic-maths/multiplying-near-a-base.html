Multiplying numbers close to 100 or 1000

निखिलं नवतश्चरमं दशतः Nikhilaṃ Navataścaramaṃ Daśataḥ “all from 9 and the last from 10”, a sutra of Vedic maths. The differences from the base are complements: all from 9 and the last from 10.

When two numbers are both close to the same round number, the base, you can multiply them using only how far each one is from it. This is the Nikhilam method, from the same sutra as subtracting from 1000.

The method

  1. Pick the base: the power of 10 both numbers are close to (10, 100, 1000…).
  2. Write how far each number is from it: minus below the base, plus above. 97 is −3, 96 is −4.
  3. Left part: one number with the other’s difference, crosswise: 97 − 4 = 93. The other way round, 96 − 3, gives the same.
  4. Right part: multiply the two differences: (−3) × (−4) = 12.
  5. The right part has as many digits as the base has zeros: two for 100. Fill it with a 0 in front, or carry the extra to the left part.

Worked examples

97 × 96

  1. Both are close to 100. Write how far each is from it.97: −3, 96: −4
  2. Left part: one number with the other's difference (crosswise).97 − 4 = 93
  3. Right part: multiply the differences.(−3) × (−4) = 12

97 × 96 = 93 | 12 = 9312

104 × 112

  1. Both are close to 100. Write how far each is from it.104: +4, 112: +12
  2. Left part: one number with the other's difference (crosswise).104 + 12 = 116
  3. Right part: multiply the differences.4 × 12 = 48

104 × 112 = 116 | 48 = 11648

88 × 89

  1. Both are close to 100. Write how far each is from it.88: −12, 89: −11
  2. Left part: one number with the other's difference (crosswise).88 − 11 = 77
  3. Right part: multiply the differences.(−12) × (−11) = 132
  4. The right part can only have 2 digits: carry 1 to the left part.77 | 132 = 78 | 32

88 × 89 = 78 | 32 = 7832

103 × 98

  1. Both are close to 100. Write how far each is from it.103: +3, 98: −2
  2. Left part: one number with the other's difference (crosswise).103 − 2 = 101
  3. Right part: multiply the differences.3 × (−2) = −6
  4. The right part is below zero: take 1 from the left part, which adds 100 to the right part.101 | −6 = 100 | 94

103 × 98 = 100 | 94 = 10094

996 × 993

  1. Both are close to 1000. Write how far each is from it.996: −4, 993: −7
  2. Left part: one number with the other's difference (crosswise).996 − 7 = 989
  3. Right part: multiply the differences.(−4) × (−7) = 28
  4. Give the right part 3 digits.028

996 × 993 = 989 | 028 = 989028

Why it works

Write the numbers as 100 − x and 100 − y. Then (100 − x)(100 − y) = 100 × (100 − x − y) + xy. The first part, 100 − x − y, is one number minus the other’s difference, and it counts hundreds; the second part, xy, is the product of the differences.

Above the base the same algebra gives (100 + x)(100 + y) = 100 × (100 + x + y) + xy, and with one above and one below, the product of the differences is negative.

Watch out

  • It always gives the right answer, but it is only quick when both numbers are close to the same base: for 97 × 12 it doesn’t help.
  • One above and one below gives a negative right part: borrow 1 from the left part, worth 100 in the right part.
  • Far from the base, the right part grows: 88 × 89 gives 77 | 132, and the 1 carries.

Bases like 50

For numbers near 50, use 50 as a working base: it is 100 ÷ 2. Work as if the base were 100, then halve the left part. (The book’s sub-sutra Ānurūpyeṇa, “proportionately”, covers bases like this.)

48 × 46

  1. Use 50 as the base: it is 100 ÷ 2.48: −2, 46: −4
  2. Left part, crosswise as before.48 − 4 = 44
  3. The base is half of 100, so halve the left part.44 ÷ 2 = 22
  4. Right part: multiply the differences.(−2) × (−4) = 08

48 × 46 = 22 | 08 = 2208
