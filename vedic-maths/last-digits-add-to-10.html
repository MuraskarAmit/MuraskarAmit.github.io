Multiplying when the last digits add up to 10

अन्त्ययोर्दशकेऽपि Antyayordaśake’pi “when the last digits add up to ten”, a sub-sutra of Vedic maths.

The sub-sutra Antyayordaśake’pi, “when the last digits add up to ten”, covers pairs like 63 × 67 or 112 × 118: the same front, and last digits that add up to 10. It is the squares ending in 5 method made general: 75 × 75 is the pair where both last digits are 5.

The method

  1. Check the pair: the same front (6 and 6) and last digits that add up to 10 (3 + 7).
  2. Left part: the front times one more than itself: 6 × 7 = 42.
  3. Right part: multiply the last digits, and write two digits: 3 × 7 = 21, and 1 × 9 = 09.
  4. Put them side by side: 4221.

Worked examples

63 × 67

  1. Both start with 6, and the last digits add up to 10.3 + 7 = 10
  2. Left part: 6 times one more than itself.6 × 7 = 42
  3. Right part: multiply the last digits, and write two digits.3 × 7 = 21

63 × 67 = 42 | 21 = 4221

81 × 89

  1. Both start with 8, and the last digits add up to 10.1 + 9 = 10
  2. Left part: 8 times one more than itself.8 × 9 = 72
  3. Right part: multiply the last digits, and write two digits.1 × 9 = 09

81 × 89 = 72 | 09 = 7209

112 × 118

  1. Both start with 11, and the last digits add up to 10.2 + 8 = 10
  2. Left part: 11 times one more than itself.11 × 12 = 132
  3. Right part: multiply the last digits, and write two digits.2 × 8 = 16

112 × 118 = 132 | 16 = 13216

Why it works

Write the numbers as 10a + b and 10a + c, with b + c = 10. Then (10a + b)(10a + c) = 100a² + 10a(b + c) + bc = 100a² + 100a + bc = 100 × a(a + 1) + bc.

Watch out

  • The fronts must be the same: 63 × 77 doesn’t work this way.
  • Always write the right part with two digits: 81 × 89 is 72 | 09, not 72 | 9.
  • It stretches to the last two digits adding up to 100, with four digits on the right: 192 × 108 is 1 × 2 = 2, then 92 × 8 = 0736: 20736.
