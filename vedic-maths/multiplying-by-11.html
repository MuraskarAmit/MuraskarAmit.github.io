Multiplying by 11

ऊर्ध्वतिर्यग्भ्याम् Ūrdhva-tiryagbhyām “vertically and crosswise”, a sutra of Vedic maths. Multiplying by 11 is a special case of this sutra.

Multiplying by 11 is one of the best-known shortcuts in Vedic maths classes, though Tirthaji’s book has no section on it. It is vertically and crosswise with 11, where every crosswise sum is just two neighbouring digits added together.

The method

  1. The last digit of the number is the last digit of the answer.
  2. Moving left, add each digit to its neighbour on the right.
  3. The first digit of the number starts the answer.
  4. A sum of 10 or more keeps its last digit and carries 1 to the left.

Worked examples

253 × 11

  1. The last digit stays.3
  2. Add each digit to its neighbour on the right, moving left.5 + 3 = 8, 2 + 5 = 7
  3. The first digit stays.2
  4. Put them side by side.2 | 7 | 8 | 3

253 × 11 = 2783

78 × 11

  1. The last digit stays.8
  2. Add each digit to its neighbour on the right, moving left.7 + 8 = 15
  3. The first digit stays.7
  4. Put them side by side.7 | 15 | 8
  5. Carry from the right, keeping one digit in each part.8; 15: keep 5, carry 1; 7 + 1 = 8 → 858

78 × 11 = 858

4729 × 11

  1. The last digit stays.9
  2. Add each digit to its neighbour on the right, moving left.2 + 9 = 11, 7 + 2 = 9, 4 + 7 = 11
  3. The first digit stays.4
  4. Put them side by side.4 | 11 | 9 | 11 | 9
  5. Carry from the right, keeping one digit in each part.9; 11: keep 1, carry 1; 9 + 1 = 10: keep 0, carry 1; 11 + 1 = 12: keep 2, carry 1; 4 + 1 = 5 → 52019

4729 × 11 = 52019

Why it works

Times 11 is times 10 plus times 1: 253 × 11 = 2530 + 253. Write the two numbers one above the other, and each place of the answer adds a digit to its neighbour.

Watch out

  • Carry from the right: in 78 × 11 the middle sum is 15, so keep 5 and carry 1 to the 7: 858.
  • For 111, 1111 and so on the same idea adds three or four neighbours at a time, and the carries pile up faster.
